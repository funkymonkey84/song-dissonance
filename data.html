The pattern holds when genre mix is held fixed

Rock and metal fell from 54% of songs in the 1970s to 18.5% in the 2010s while hip hop and rap rose from 8% to 57%, which could have created a false trend. After recalculating each decade with the same genre mix, the rates were 18.8% (1970s), 15.6% (1990s), and 9.3% (2010s). The decline remains. Results were also similar at Positiveness cutoffs of 60 and 75.

Checking the labels by hand

Both measures are automated, so I checked 90 randomly drawn songs that the computer had flagged as mismatches (30 per decade, one song per artist, seed 42).

The lyric labels held up. The music score agreed with my judgment less often in later decades, so the “upbeat” label may be less reliable for newer songs. That would likely make the real decline larger, not smaller, but the sample is small (about ±15 points) and I did not check songs the computer left unflagged.

Data and methods

Source: the Kaggle dataset “500K+ Spotify Songs with Lyrics, Emotions & More” (file: final_milliondataset_BERT_500K_revised.json). Lyrics are not reproduced here.

Three versions of the data

Original dataset364,376The source file, with lyrics. Not hosted here.

Cleaning steps

  • One malformed line was skipped, “angry” was merged into “anger,” and songs without a valid emotion label were removed.
  • Duplicates (same artist and title) were removed. Live versions or alternate spellings may remain.
  • The release year was extracted and grouped into decades. Songs before 1950 were excluded because there are too few.
  • For the capped version, songs were shuffled (seed 42) and the first 25 per artist per decade were kept.

Definitions

  • Upbeat: Positiveness of 67 or higher on a 0 to 100 scale, which appears to correspond to Spotify's valence.
  • Negative lyrics: an emotion label of sadness, anger, or fear.
  • Mismatch: a song that is both.

Limitations

  • The dataset leans toward recent music. The 2010s hold about half the songs, and older songs are the ones that stayed available.
  • Genre tags are unreliable. Some songs carry clearly wrong tags, and genre is a secondary check only.
  • Some years may be reissue dates, which would blur the decades. This has not been measured.
  • Both measures are automated estimates, and the music score agreed with a human listener less often for newer songs.
  • Three snapshots, so nothing can be said about what happened between them.
  • Context explains, it does not prove. The data cannot show why the pattern changed.
